Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines a search step with a large language
model. Instead of relying only on what the model memorised during training, the
system first retrieves relevant passages from a private knowledge base and then
asks the model to answer using those passages as context.

An embedding is a fixed-length vector of floating point numbers that represents
the meaning of a piece of text. Texts with similar meaning produce vectors that
point in similar directions. The embedding model nomic-embed-text produces
768-dimensional vectors.

Cosine similarity measures the angle between two vectors: it is the dot product
divided by the product of their lengths. A score of 1 means identical direction,
0 means unrelated, and -1 means opposite.

A vector store such as Qdrant indexes millions of embeddings with an approximate
nearest neighbour algorithm called HNSW, so the most similar chunks can be found
in milliseconds without comparing the query against every stored vector.

The ingestion pipeline loads documents, splits them into overlapping chunks,
embeds each chunk and upserts the vectors with their text as payload. The query
pipeline embeds the question, searches the vector store for the top-k chunks and
places them in the prompt sent to the language model.
